After going through the whole thing I talked about on my last post, consisting of understanding where and exactly how much I fucked myself over with my own ambition, I took myself on a journey of finding out how to go back to having fun, and it... worked. I owe a lot of it to this dude on youtube who reminded me about [the power of pen and paper](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_QVYfPnNOg4), in fact. It was basically what I was trying to do this with this blog, but works a lot better in my case. I figure because failing is a thing better done privately. This one here is better kept as a diary of efforts, of sorts.

For a few months now I have kept a little unlined notebook, soft black cover, where everything goes. Absolute freedom. It's a refuge of sorts. A safe place for everything that might not make the cut, for the things that one day might. The best thing about it, though, might be the fact that it reminds me of reality: there's some really good things there, perfectly nestled against absolute crap ideas. The good makes me happy, but doesn't make me too cocky. The bad loses it's power to bring me down. Everything gains partners, becomes possibility: play.

The return to reality also came with a more literal one. Technology isn't fun anymore, and so I left whatever I still had of social media, and I'm trying to spend less time watching things on YouTube. It's still difficult. We're all addicted. Reading has helped, and the joy of being able to return to reading has helped even more. Ever since I decided to stop spending so much time online I finished two books, wrote a lot more, watched more movies than I had in the last year, and generally started feeling like a human being who own her own life, again. I even went back to the gym, will you look at that.

There's something of a return to the origin, in this. A return to bare matter, to what is most baseline about ourselves, the things we can't forget because without them we don't exist. Whether there's life on mars or not, I have to eat, you know. There's such an undeniable and indelible thing about this, isn't there? It's so beautiful, too. The solidity of that which is *physical*, or meeting people and drinking coffee and feeling graphite scratch on cellulose, and the truth of that which is *fact*.

As a road towards something, I've been on it for years, without truly realizing it, and it's brought me back to analogue photography, and back to film when it comes to cinema, too. And to writing; back to the hearing my characters, and back to enjoying my own scenes, the trial and error of it all.

It was March, the last time i actually wrote a scene for the feature I last talked about. I remember feeling so bad about what was on the screen that I kinda rage quit. When I revisited it, a few weeks ago, it was in need of a fix, sure, but not at all as disastrous as I remember. I laughed at my own jokes, having forgotten them. Thought of better ones. Realized that other scenes needed adjusting. Left myself a good hook for the next ones, something to go on. And I'm excited to go back to it, work through the next set of problems.

My little new notebook has pictures of things that remind me of the story. It makes me silly and happy when I look at it. I've also watched *Anatomie d'une chute*, got obsessed with Justine Triet's directing style. It was the first time I've felt truly inspired in a long time. Sad to have lost it, yes, but how lucky—I've recently said this words about something else, to someone else, and am now made to learn my own lesson—*how lucky* it is to experience a first twice.

This would all be tangential to writing at best, if it wasn't for the fact that the story touches on these topics, in a way or another. A return to self. The only place from which we can truly create, I think.
