### A theory by any other name

A *change of frames of reference*.

That's the most important thing the people who have tried to explain this to me usually fail to truly impart. They tell cute stories about twins and trains at the speed of light or whatever, but never truly explain that this is *defining* when it comes to relativity. The name "theory of relativity" became so associated with time, specifically, that I think I completely lost the view of what it's really about.

What relativity is, is a theory about *perspective*. About frames of reference, and what happens when you jump from nome to another. You cannot have relativity talking about a single referential. You are necessarily talking about a single observable phenomena, in some way or another: two observers at different enough speeds, when asked about their motion, cannot agree on anything. Space is weird, time is mush. They can't agree on how long it's been, or how long they've traveled.

Asterisk: they do agree on something, and one thing only. The speed of light is the same no matter what, too.

We start from there. From two observers in motion. And from there the whole things unravels quite easily when you start to understand what does it means to pick one or the other as the referential.

### A better way of thinking through it

Whenever someone tries to explain relativity to someone else they start with a pretty classic thought experiment. What if you were on a train that was moving close to the speed of light? So many weird things happen! Wow! Time becomes weird! Space shrinks! Wow! It's rElAtIvE, so cool.

That is the wrong way to do it, it is virtually useless, and I hate everyone who has ever *thunk* it was a good way to teach anyone anything. There's no way to visualize being at the speed of light. There's nowhere to come out from unless you already know a lot of the physics involved in it. Even if you do, it's just entirely idiotic because you're throwing facts on people that they have no trust for, an therefore no intuition for, either.

You have to start with very concrete facts, and walk through them very slowly. Like we were talking on the section before: we're considering here two different frames of reference. That's our starting axiom.

Now, you need to go back to the very basics and come back to a single fact: speed is distance over time. This is not even calculus. This is basic physics.

We start from something we all probably know—

$|v| = \frac {d}{t}$

—and if you're thinking about speed as we know it, then yes, we have a number for how fast light moves.

$c = 299,792,458 \text{ m/s}$ 

The weirdness of it all emerges naturally from these first few premises, that we have confirmed with experimentation: 

- The equation describes what speed is;
- The speed of light is an absolute, which mean: it's the maximum something can move at.

It's possible to explore these starting points  later as their own thing, or before arriving at relativity—which is also something mathematicians and physicists love to proudly fail at: connecting knowledge to itself.

But in my hatred, I digress. What is important is that when you're hammering down that the speed of light is always the same and it never changes—and it's the most constant-est thing ever, and the only thing we know for sure that is fixed, and yada yada—, you *have* to remind people that speed is not a *thing*. It's a *relationship* between things. 

I'll say it again for the people in the back: the one thing that is fixed in the universe is not a concrete thing. It's not a distance. Not even the *idea of distance*. Not time, or the idea of it. It is a *relationship* between two things: distance and time.

And if that is constant, then the other side of our speed equation can be whatever the fuck respects that result. There's not one single set of numbers that result in this speed becoming what it is, there are infinite possibilities, and they are all valid as long as we get that magic number.

Space or time could consist entirely of a banana, a can of sardines, a cup of coffee, the tears of your enemies, the number 5—and the universe would be fine with it as long as the speed of light stayed fixed. Because it would be fixed anyway. That's what fixed mean. Everything else revolves around it, not the other way round.

$\frac {banana}{5} = \text{still fucking }  299,792,458 \text{ m/s}$

For now, this is just shenanigans. Math is lawless and will allow bananas or sardines to become a valid state of reality if I want to, but this has real implications to the empirical fact of the universe. 

At these speeds, anything goes. Space and time, ($d$ and $t$) can take any value, because reality organizes itself around the fixedness of that *speed*. Therefore, speed is not something that emerges out of space and time. *Space and time emerge out of movement*.

This is the most important part of the comprehension of relativity, as far as I'm concerned.

Everything organizes itself around $c$. 

It is the center of our universe. Time dilation is nothing but $t$ assuming different values to respect the fixedness of $c$. The same for length contraction. It's space squishing to keep $c$ from breaking.



### The stupidest name

Our next axiom is the following: this the fastest something can move in the universe, and you can only go that fast if you're massless. How we know that? We tried shooting a bunch of things real fast. Light happens to be massless, and it was the only thing that reached those speeds.

Light is also the only thing truly confirmed to be massless, so, so far. But the name "speed of light" treats light as special, when this not about light at all. Theoretically, any other massless particle could move at that same speed.

The think about $c$ is that, once you start thinking about the consequences of the limit of reality being a speed, the idea of *speed* becomes quickly irrelevant.
